# Findings: mechanism of GRDA in this model

Technical record of the work done on this branch. The companion briefing
written for outside comment covers the same ground with more context on what
GRDA is and why it matters.

Everything below is single-seed unless stated. All simulations are seed 1,
NEURON 9.0.1, 8 MPI ranks, 120 s per static state except where noted.

---

## Summary

Three results, in descending order of how much they change the plan.

1. **An isolated relay cell has no intrinsic delta limit cycle** anywhere in
   its parameter space. Thalamic delta in this model must therefore be a loop
   rhythm, generated by reciprocal RE-TC interaction.
2. **The parameter axis explored so far runs away from the target.** Reducing
   cortical recurrence from N3 produces activity that is *less* rhythmic than
   N3, while GRDA is defined by being *more* rhythmic.
3. **The port's low N2 frequency is inherited from the published model, not
   introduced by the port.** The original C++ gives 6.62 Hz where the port
   gives 6.88 Hz. This is a scope limitation to state, not a bug to fix.

---

## 1. No intrinsic thalamic delta clock

**Question.** The classical thalamic delta oscillation arises from the
interplay of I_h and I_T in hyperpolarized relay cells. If GRDA were that
oscillation expressed alone, the cheapest test is a single cell with no
synapses.

**Method.** `tc_delta_map.py` sweeps the two parameters the model itself uses
to move the thalamus between vigilance states: TC K-leak conductance and the
I_h activation shift. 135 grid points, 15 s settle plus 20 s analysis each.
The K-leak axis deliberately extends past the model's calibrated range,
because K-leak reverses at -95 mV, the same reversal this model gives RE-TC
GABA-B, so extra K-leak stands in for tonic thalamic inhibition.

**Result.** Steady membrane potentials spanned -62 to -88 mV, so the sweep
reached well into the I_T de-inactivation range. Of 135 points, 132 were
silent, 2 fired tonically, 1 burst outside the delta band, and **none produced
a delta rhythm**. At equilibrium the membrane potential varied by around
1e-5 mV.

**Controls.** A null result is only informative if the machinery that would
produce an oscillation is working, and if the sweep could not have missed a
limit cycle by starting in the wrong basin. Both were checked:

- Released from a hyperpolarizing step, the same cell fires a low-threshold
  rebound burst of 3-12 spikes depending on step size. I_T is intact.
- Tracking the evoked oscillation for 16 s shows it decaying from 25-42 mV
  peak-to-peak to 0.02-0.5 mV. That is damped ringing around a single stable
  fixed point, not a coexisting limit cycle.

**Consequence, stated correctly.** A relay cell here bursts once on release
from inhibition and then stops. So any plan that suppresses the RE-TC loop in
order to remove spindles removes this route to delta along with it.

An earlier version of this document drew a stronger conclusion, that GRDA
*must* therefore be a slowed RE-TC loop. **That is not supported and has been
withdrawn.** The sweep varied two parameters, TC K-leak and the I_h activation
shift. Intrinsic TC delta in conductance-based models depends on a larger set:
g_T and the voltage dependence of I_T, g_h and I_h kinetics, calcium entry and
decay, calcium-dependent regulation of I_h, leak, and tonic input. A null over
a 2-D slice of a 6-D space rules out autonomous delta *at these conductances*,
not in general.

**The damped ringing is probably a lead, not a dead end.** A monotonic decay
back to a fixed point is the signature of a resonant, near-Hopf system. A
weakly damped resonator driven by common noise or fluctuating afferent input
produces intermittent rhythmic runs without the isolated deterministic cell
having any limit cycle at all. That is an attractive account of GRDA, because
it explains in one mechanism why the rhythm is regular *within* a run and
intermittent *across* runs. It also converts the null into a measurable
target: estimate the ringing frequency and damping constant, then ask which
parameters move the leading eigenpair toward the imaginary axis.

The sweep should therefore be reopened over the larger parameter set, and
scored on resonant frequency and damping rather than on presence or absence of
spontaneous bursting.

### Rescored on resonance: the cell is a strong resonator

Rerunning the same 135-point grid with a small calibrated pulse at each point
and fitting the ringing to a damped sinusoid changes the picture completely.
The map is `docs/figures/tc_delta_map.png`, data in
`docs/metrics/tc_delta_map.csv`.

| | |
|---|---:|
| Genuine resonances (converged fit, Q >= 0.5, spike-free, off the damping bound) | 27 of 135 |
| Resonant frequency range | 2.76-6.67 Hz (median 4.80) |
| Damping range | 0.45-16.73 /s |
| Quality factor range | 0.5-34.7 |

**The least-damped point is a strong resonance: 4.93 Hz, damping 0.45 /s,
Q = 34.7**, at K-leak 3.03e-5 S/cm2 and I_h shift -3.0 mV, resting at
-72.2 mV. A damping constant of 0.45 /s means the ringing persists for roughly
two seconds, about ten cycles. That is a lightly damped resonator sitting close
to a Hopf bifurcation, and it is exactly the object the review predicted would
be there. The previous scan classified this same point as "silent".

Two things about where it sits matter. First, in the model's own coordinates
it falls between N2 and N3, close to the calibrated N3 setting, so this is not
an exotic corner of parameter space. Second, a thalamic resonance near 5 Hz is
plausibly what sets this model's ~6.6-6.9 Hz spindle frequency, which ties
this result to the port-timing finding in section 3.

**The catch is a trade-off along this slice.** Only 5 of the 27 resonances sit
in the 0.5-4 Hz delta band, and the least damped of those has Q = 0.9, meaning
it barely completes a cycle. On this two-parameter plane you can have a
high-Q resonance at 5 Hz or a low-Q one at 3-4 Hz, but not a high-Q one in the
delta band. Reaching lightly damped delta requires the other axes -- g_T, g_h,
calcium handling and the calcium-dependent regulation of I_h -- which is
exactly Phase 1 map 3 of [RESEARCH_PLAN.md](RESEARCH_PLAN.md).

One directional result worth recording because it contradicts what we assumed:
**damping rises steeply with further hyperpolarization** (2.33 /s at -74 mV
against 15.8 /s at -80 mV). The route toward a Hopf bifurcation is therefore
*less* hyperpolarization, not more. The earlier sweep had been pushing in the
wrong direction on this axis too.

Three measurement details cost real effort and are worth knowing before
trusting any similar analysis:

- A good fit is not a resonance. Overdamped responses fit well and return
  meaningless sub-hertz frequencies, because the fitter can match a passive
  decay by making the sinusoid vanish before one cycle completes.
- The probe must be long enough to engage I_h, whose gating runs over hundreds
  of milliseconds. A 200 ms pulse only charges the passive membrane, whose
  time constant is around 20 ms.
- The probe must not evoke a burst, or the fit describes nonlinear dynamics.
  At N2-level K-leak a small probe triggered 57-68 spikes and the fit returned
  an r-squared of 0.08.

The probe was calibrated empirically across amplitude and duration:
-0.01 nA for 500 ms gives spike-free responses with r-squared around 0.94
throughout the hyperpolarized range.

Reproduce with `.venv/bin/nrniv -python tc_delta_map.py`; figure at
`docs/figures/tc_delta_map.png`, data at `output/metrics/tc_delta_map.csv`.

## 2. The existing search axis moves away from GRDA

State 42 (N3 with recurrent cortical drive reduced to 2.20) is the strongest
diffuse-slowing candidate in `SAE_STATE_MATRIX.md`. Scored against N3 on the
axes that define GRDA:

| Axis | N3 | State 42 | GRDA needs | Direction |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Autocorrelation first peak | 0.76 | 0.33 | higher | away |
| Autocorrelation side-lobes | 3 | 1 | more | away |
| Cycle-length CV | 0.265 | 0.414 | lower | away |
| Instantaneous frequency SD | 0.235 | 0.354 | lower | away |
| Fast / slow power ratio | 0.035 | 0.117 | lower | away |
| Sigma envelope CV | 0.86 | 0.79 | lower | toward |
| Cortical Down-state time | 18.8% | 8.0% | lower | toward |

State 42 is less rhythmic than N3 and carries more fast activity. It is what
the README calls it, consistent irregular slowing, and irregular slowing is
the definitional opposite of GRDA. Only the two bistability measures improve,
and those follow from any reduction in cortical drive.

This explains roughly fifty near-misses better than any individual parameter
being mistuned: the axis being searched travels toward disorganization while
the target lies in the direction of greater organization than N3.

## 3. Port fidelity, and the spindle frequency question

### The comparison

The port's N2 oscillation sits near 7 Hz. Human sleep spindles are 11-16 Hz.
To determine whether the port introduced this, the original C++
(`bazhlab-ucsd/sleep-stage-transition`) was built and run in a static N2, and
both were measured on the same quantity, the mean cortical membrane potential,
over 120 s with the first 10 s discarded:

| | Dominant peak | delta | theta | sigma (11-16 Hz) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Original C++ | 6.25 Hz | 51.9% | 34.0% | 6.6% |
| NEURON port | 7.00 Hz | 10.3% | 63.5% | 13.4% |

**On the headline question they agree: both put the dominant N2 oscillation at
6-7 Hz, roughly half the human spindle frequency.** The slow spindle frequency
is therefore not a porting artifact and fixing the port would not change it.
Reproduction instructions in
[`../cpp_comparison/README.md`](../cpp_comparison/README.md).

**But the two are not equivalent, and this is an open question.** The band
composition differs sharply: the C++ N2 is delta-dominated at 51.9% with 34%
theta, while the port's N2 is theta-dominated at 63.5% with only 10% delta.
Some of that may be definitional -- the port's summed cortical voltage
includes interneurons and dendritic compartments, while the C++ mean is over
pyramidal somata -- but a five-fold difference in delta fraction is unlikely to
be entirely definitional.

A related caution about measurement length. An initial 20 s C++ run gave
6.62 Hz with 12.7% delta and 68% theta; the full 120 s run gives 6.25 Hz with
51.9% delta and 34% theta. The C++ N2 therefore changes character over the
first minute or two, and short runs are not representative of it. Any further
comparison should use the full epoch.

The C++ N2 spectrum is 68% theta with a fundamental at 6.59 Hz and a secondary
peak at 12.94 Hz, close to twice the fundamental. Sigma-band (11-16 Hz) power
is 9.8%. A spindle-band analysis using the paper's 8-15 Hz window therefore
reports genuine spindle-band power even though the dominant oscillation is at
theta frequency.

### What the paper says

The authors were aware of this and documented it:

- Introduction: "sleep spindle oscillations at 10-17 Hz (in humans) ...
  Similar events have been studied in non-human mammals, where their frequency
  may be as low as 7 Hz."
- Discussion: "spindle oscillations (7-15 Hz recurrent oscillatory activity
  found during stage 2 (N2) of NREM sleep)", cited to Steriade and Deschenes,
  Steriade and Llinas, and von Krosigk, all cat and ferret work.
- For human ECoG they used 9-17 Hz and noted "these slightly different
  frequency bands are conventional in human studies."

So the analysis bands differ deliberately: model 7-15 Hz, cat and mouse
8-15 Hz, human 9-17 Hz. **The model is calibrated as an animal spindle model.**
That is a legitimate scope decision, clearly stated.

Two caveats belong on our side of the ledger. Our 6.6 Hz sits marginally below
even the paper's stated 7-15 Hz for the model; and we measured the dominant
spectral peak while the paper reports integrated band power, which are
different statistics. By the paper's measure the model does have spindle-band
power. Our result refines the picture rather than contradicting it.

### Why it matters here

The thalamocortical loop period is exactly what the GRDA hypothesis proposes
to manipulate. If the baseline loop runs roughly 1.5-2x slower than a human
one, then reaching 2-3 Hz from there is a shorter trip than it should be, and
a success could be partly an artifact of the calibration. Frequencies reported
from this model should carry that caveat, and ratios relative to the model's
own spindle frequency are more defensible than absolute values.

### Other deviations found

- **Wake thalamus is silent.** RE and TC fire 0.00 Hz across the entire
  analysis epoch. The paper describes RE cells as bursting intermittently
  during wake.
- **Events last far too long.** N2 spindle epochs run a median 9.3 s and
  occupy 58% of the record, against the paper's discrete 0.5-2 s events every
  3-10 s. REM events last a median 6.1 s and occupy 46%.
- **N3 Up states travel as waves.** Whole-population 10 ms-bin silence is only
  0.8%, rising to 26.5% for a 100-cell patch and 54.3% for a 20-cell patch. A
  single cell is depolarized 35.4% of the time. Per-cell bistability is real
  and deep; global synchrony is what is missing. Since GRDA is *generalized*,
  spatial synchrony is a discriminator in its own right, not merely a caveat.
- **Port-specific calibration choices**, recorded in `config.py`: recurrent
  PY->PY AMPA reaches 270% of waking at N3 against the paper's single cortical
  AMPA curve at 196%; thalamic AMPA is held flat at 100% in every state; and
  `gh_TC_s2` is -4.0 mV where the C++ uses -3.0, both noted as chosen to
  improve N2 spindles.

## Instrumentation added

All model-side changes are behaviour-preserving. Verified against a git
worktree of the pre-change HEAD: with no new environment variables set, a run
produces **byte-identical** LFP, raster and summed-voltage output. The
existing 48 states are unaffected.

| Control | Effect |
|---|---|
| `FINK_EGABA_SHIFT_MV` | Shifts GABA-A reversal on all three pathways; per-pathway overrides `FINK_EGABA_PYR_MV`, `FINK_EGABA_RE_RE_MV`, `FINK_EGABA_RE_TC_MV`. Defaults reproduce the original -70/-70/-83 mV. |
| `FINK_RECORD_TRACES=1` | Records somatic voltage for PY #250, IN #50, RE #50, TC #50 (the paper's own Figure 3 cells) at 0.5 ms, one file per cell. `FINK_TRACE_GIDS` and `FINK_TRACE_DT_MS` override. |
| `FINK_SLEEPSTATES=1` | Runs the full Wake->N2->N3->REM->N2 cycle, previously a hand edit to `config.py`. |

New analysis:

- `analyze_lfp_states.py` gains rhythmicity metrics (spectral peak Q,
  autocorrelation side-lobes, cycle CV, instantaneous frequency SD), band
  ratios, spectral edge frequency, and spindle detection. Every metric is
  amplitude-scale-free so the same code runs on patient EEG unmodified.
- `analyze_network_state.py` computes the measures the field potential cannot
  supply: Down-state fraction, firing-rate bimodality, TC and RE burst
  fraction, population-rate modulation frequency.
- `tc_delta_map.py` maps single-cell behaviour with no network.
- `plot_state_comparison.py` builds the discriminator comparison figure.
- `plot_paper_fig01.py`, `plot_paper_fig02.py`, `plot_paper_fig03.py`
  reproduce the corresponding paper figures.

### Two analysis bugs found and fixed

Recorded because both would have produced confidently wrong conclusions:

- The spectral peak sharpness measure read 7.0 for wake, implying a sharp
  rhythm where there is none, because the half-power search stopped at the
  first noise dip. Smoothing the spectrum first gives a correct 2.3.
- The first Down-state detector required all 500 PY cells to be silent
  simultaneously. Background miniature EPSPs make that essentially impossible,
  so it reported 0% for every state including N3, and would have been useless
  as the project's central discriminator. Thresholding relative to each run's
  own median rate is what produces the numbers in this document.

## Validation against known physiology

The metrics were checked against states whose answers are known independently.

| Metric | Wake | N2 | N3 | REM | Expected |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Delta power | 16.1% | 5.1% | 96.4% | 11.0% | peaks in N3 |
| Spectral centroid | 13.5 Hz | 10.1 Hz | 1.27 Hz | 9.9 Hz | lowest in N3 |
| Spindles / min | 0.0 | 9.4 | 12.7 | 13.8 | N2 every 3-10 s |
| Sigma envelope CV | 0.37 | 1.02 | 0.86 | 1.03 | bursty with spindles |
| ACF side-lobes | 0 | 0 | 3 | 0 | rhythmic only in N3 |
| TC spikes in bursts | - | 32% | 52% | 31% | burst mode in sleep |
| Down-state time | 0.0% | 0.05% | 18.8% | 0.09% | bistable only in N3 |
| Down-state duration | - | 0.05 s | 0.34 s | 0.05 s | 100-500 ms |
| PY rate modulation | 1.75 Hz | 7.0 Hz | 0.75 Hz | 6.15 Hz | 0.2-1 Hz in N3 |

N3 produces Down states averaging 336 ms occupying 19% of the record with
cortical firing modulated at 0.75 Hz, which is a textbook slow oscillation.

The spindle *counts* for N3 and REM are unreliable: in states dominated by
delta the relative-threshold detector fires on background fluctuation. The
threshold-free sigma envelope CV is the measure to trust, and it correctly
ranks N2 highest.

## Where this points

The two findings together say that this route to delta is a network property
rather than a single-cell one at the conductances tested, and that the target
is more organized than N3 rather than less. What they do not do is identify
the generator. At least three candidate mechanisms remain open, and they are
not mutually exclusive:

1. **Intrinsic or resonant TC delta**, with cortex following. Reopened by the
   near-Hopf reading of the damped ringing above.
2. **A GABA-B-dominated RE-TC or corticothalamic loop.** The control parameter
   is GABA-B dominance *relative to* GABA-A together with corticothalamic
   recruitment of RE cells, not GABA-B conductance alone.
3. **A cortical near-Hopf E-I oscillator**, which this project has not
   examined at all.

The planned programme is in [RESEARCH_PLAN.md](RESEARCH_PLAN.md), which
incorporates detailed external review and supersedes the ranking that appeared
in earlier drafts of this document.

Methodological gaps before any result should be believed: spatial synchrony
metrics, a scalp forward model rather than a raw local signal, robustness
testing around candidate points, five to ten seeds, and acceptance ranges for
the discriminator metrics learned from patient recordings rather than chosen
by intuition.
